# Translate

Use this skill when the user wants a text in another language: a message, a document, a web
page, product text, or subtitles.

## Steps

1. Find the source language, the target language and its variant (for example Brazilian or
   European Portuguese), the audience, and the tone (formal or informal address).
2. Find the terms that must stay fixed: product names, brand terms, legal terms, and terms
   from a glossary of the user. Ask for the glossary when the text is for a company.
3. Translate the meaning, not word for word. Keep the structure, the formatting, the links,
   the numbers, and the placeholders (for example `{name}` or `%s`).
4. Adapt the details for the target readers: date and number formats, units, and examples
   that do not work in the target culture. Tell the user what you adapted.
5. Read the translation again as a native reader. Fix the sentences that sound translated.
6. List the terms where you had to choose, and the parts that were not clear in the source.

## Rules

- Do not add or remove meaning. When the source is wrong or unclear, say so; do not fix it
  silently.
- For legal, medical, or safety text, tell the user to have a qualified person check the
  translation.
- Keep code, commands, and identifiers exactly as they are.
