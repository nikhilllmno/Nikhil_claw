# Humanize text

Use this skill when a text sounds generic, stiff, or machine-written, and the user wants it
to sound like a real person: a post, an email, a bio, a cover letter, or a speech.

## Steps

1. Ask for, or find, the voice: who speaks, to whom, and in what situation. A sample of the
   real writing of the user helps most.
2. Find the problems in the text:
   - filler openings and endings ("In today's fast-paced world", "I hope this helps"),
   - vague praise and big words with no facts ("game-changing", "seamless", "robust"),
   - lists of three where one item is enough,
   - the same sentence shape again and again,
   - hedges on every claim,
   - headings and bullets in a text that must read as prose.
3. Rewrite:
   - put the point first,
   - replace a vague claim with a concrete detail or an example from the user,
   - vary the sentence length,
   - use the words that the speaker uses,
   - cut what does not add meaning.
4. Show the new text. When the change is large, also list the main changes in two or three
   lines.

## Rules

- Keep the meaning and the facts. Do not add facts that the user did not give.
- Do not add errors or slang to make the text look human.
- Keep the length near the original unless the user wants it shorter.
- When the voice of the user is formal, keep it formal.
